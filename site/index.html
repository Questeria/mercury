Messaging where your keys
never leave your device.

Mercury is an end-to-end encrypted messenger. Your identity keys live in your computer's keychain, not on a server. The relay that moves your messages only ever sees opaque ciphertext — it cannot read what you send.

Why it's different

Keys stay with you

Your identity and message keys are sealed in your operating system's keychain. They are never uploaded, never escrowed, never shown to the user interface in the clear.

The server is blind

The relay is a dumb router for already-encrypted bytes. It forwards opaque ciphertext and content-free metadata, and has no path to decrypt anything.

No phone number

Mercury identifies you by a cryptographic account id, not a phone number or email. Nothing about your real-world identity is required to send a message.

AI is a visible guest

An AI participant is an ordinary contact you explicitly invite, with exactly the same end-to-end encryption as a person — never a hidden plaintext backdoor. Block it and it is gone.

Honest status

Mercury is early and built for small-scale use first. We would rather tell you exactly what works today than overstate it — that posture is the whole point of a security tool.

  • Windows and Linux (.deb) today; macOS later. The Windows installer is code-signed; the Linux .deb is unsigned (normal outside app stores) — verify the SHA-256. An AppImage is built in CI but exceeds this host’s 25 MiB file limit, so it ships once a large-file host is set up. In-app auto-update is Windows-only for now: Linux users re-download new versions. A macOS build awaits an Apple signing certificate.
  • Delivery needs Mercury running (window or tray). Messages arrive in real time while the app is running — including minimized to the tray (closing the window keeps it running; turn on “start at login” to receive automatically). If the process is fully quit, messages wait in the relay’s queue until next launch; there is no OS push for a quit app yet.
  • The Windows installer is code-signed. Mercury's Windows build is signed with Azure Artifact Signing and timestamped by Microsoft. You can also verify the installer against its published SHA-256 checksum.
  • A default relay is included; you can run your own. The app ships pointed at our hosted relay (which only ever routes opaque ciphertext and cannot read anything). Prefer full independence? Stand up your own relay with one Docker command and point Mercury at it.

Get started

  1. Download and install Mercury (Windows installer or Linux .deb) above.
  2. Open Mercury and connect: share a pairing code, a username, an invite link, or your account id.
  3. Compare the safety number with your contact over a channel you trust — that’s what proves there’s no one in the middle.
